The **Tyrean** are a [[Culture]] in [[Northern Hearth]] which reside mostly in the [[Republic of Tiereth]] and the [[Aristocracy of Tiereth]]. Most Tyrean are [[Madrian]].

They don’t strictly hold to the even lettered names that [[Caelish]] do, but they do believe the firstborn is unlucky, believed for the many deaths of firstborns as they grew accustomed to the climate and sicknesses.

They hold a belief that a single person is intrinsically lonely, so they need a pair. Thus, hold that marriage should be permanent (calling them Bondmates instead). This permanent bonding does not extend to large friend groups, as those are largely self-sufficient. But they do place a particular weight on the importance of friendship.

Unlike the [[Caelish]] with their focus on True Names, or the Elden with their focus on heritage, the Tyrean place the most weight on birthright. The firstborn has the most importance and weight placed on them, seen with their naming conventions. The firstborn is expected (no matter the gender) to lead the family and make the hard decisions once their father passes the torch to them. Because they have survived the hardships of the Mountains first, they deserve to lead.

There are five Pursuits that a Tyrean can be expected to follow: Advancement, Academics, Leadership, Stewarding, and Defense.

In their Pursuit of Defense, the people of Tiereth formed the Wargraves, commanders responsible for border patrol in the Kapechi Range. They mostly surround Kalidom, but some venture to Rakuken to run missives between the capitals, or are assigned duty to a smaller city if necessary.

## Linguistics
Inspired by China and Japan.

Some of Carlen’s fascination with 2 bled over into Tiereth. The Tyrean people hold a balance between Mountain and Sea, and have a dyad language where words can have two meanings depending on the inflection they’re spoken with, like two sides of one coin.

Many Tyrean families name their children after an ascending pattern of letters, starting with three. So the firstborn would have the three letters in their name, the secondborn has four letters, the third has five, and so on. They also believe that each numbered child as a different purpose and will achieve different things.

First is strongest though unlucky, second always falls short, third is pitied, fourth is the sweet spot and often accomplished; anything beyond fifth is considered fodder.
